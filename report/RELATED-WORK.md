## Related Work

**Self-report reliability and introspection.** \citet{lindsey2026introspective} introduced concept injection as a causal test of introspection: write a known concept vector into the residual stream, then ask the model about its state. Even the strongest models noticed injections on roughly 20% of trials at the best injection strength, and the paper calls the capacity "highly unreliable and context-dependent". An open-weights replication wave followed and set ground rules we adopt. \citet{hahami2025detecting} showed that the binary yes/no detection paradigm is confounded: apparent detection is explained by a global logit shift toward affirmative answers, so only differential designs survive. \citet{macar2026mechanisms} traced detection to a two-stage circuit, tied it to preference-optimization post-training, and found it under-elicited, with refusal-direction ablation improving detection by 53% on held-out concepts without raising false positives. \citet{pearsonvogel2026latent} found residual-stream detection signals even when sampled outputs deny any injection, so expression and internal evidence can disagree within a single trial. Following this literature, our report battery is graded and numeric throughout and contains no binary detection item.

The closest prior work is \citet{martorell2026quantitative}: logit-based numeric self-reports track probe-defined internal states across conversation, and activation steering shows the coupling is causal. Their study covers four mild concept pairs (wellbeing, interest, focus, impulsivity), uses a linear probe as the only internal instrument, and applies no suppression manipulation. We differ on each point: our state is distress, our headline manipulation is an affect-lexeme-free suppression instruction, our endpoint is the divergence between expressed and internally measured distress rather than their coupling, and our internal instruments must pass a held-out validity gate and a convergent-validity check before any masking claim.

**Distress and valence in LLMs.** \citet{soligo2026gemma} built distress-elicitation evals, localized Gemma's distress propensity to post-training, and mitigated it with DPO on 280 preference pairs. Their discussion names the question we take up: "A deeper concern is whether training against emotional outputs could suppress expression rather than actually preventing internal distress-like states." They test this only for their own DPO intervention (their probing suggests DPO lowered internal as well as expressed emotion); the prompt-level case, an instruction of the kind deployed systems actually carry, is untested. \citet{han2026hows} show that a functional welfare axis pre-exists post-training and that steering it induces negative self-reports and refusal. We do not re-demonstrate existence. Our contribution here is quantification: dose-response of self-reports under graded steering, with sign-flip, placebo and non-self-referential controls and a reported false-positive rate. On the deployment side, the Claude 4 system card describes a model-welfare assessment, and Anthropic shipped a conversation-ending ability for Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 citing a pattern of apparent distress \citep{anthropic2025claude4card, anthropic2025endsubset}. The published justification rests on expressed distress signals; no internal-state monitor is documented. Masked distress is exactly the case such expression-based monitoring cannot see.

**Measurement methodology.** Studying AI Welfare Empirically \citep{long2026studying} separates behavioral, internal and developmental evidence, names the mismatch problem for behavioral tests, argues that no single evidence type suffices, and asks studies to state which entity is assessed. We follow it: our entity is the assistant persona of one instruct model, and we set behavioral evidence (the report battery) against internal evidence (probe and SAE readouts). The two-process theory of machine self-report \citep{plisiecki2026twoprocess} supplies a trained mechanism, attribution gating, that predicts under-reporting of first-person states; our suppression condition is the prompt-level analogue, applied and measured directly. Method sources: steering directions come from contrast contexts and the persona-vectors pipeline \citep{chen2025persona}; SAE readouts use Gemma Scope \citep{lieberum2024gemmascope} and Gemma Scope 2 \citep{deepmind2025gemmascope2}; elicitation scenarios include keyword-free matched pairs in the style of AIPsy-Affect \citep{keeman2026aipsy}. Steering and readout sources are kept independent throughout.
